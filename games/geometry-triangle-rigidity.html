🔺 Locked or Wobbly?

Why aren't bridges built from squares?

Cranes, roof trusses, and bridge girders are built out of triangles — almost never squares. There's a real reason: a triangle with fixed side lengths can't change shape. A square can.

You're about to build some triangles from a few given clues, try to shake each one loose, and figure out exactly which clues lock a shape down — and which ones let it wobble.

🏆 You Found What Makes a Triangle Rigid

Six clue-sets, six discoveries:

  • SSS — three sides → locked
  • SAS — two sides + the angle between → locked
  • ASA / AAS — a side + two angles → locked
  • AAA — three angles only → flexible size (similar, not congruent)
  • SSA — two sides + a non-included angle → can be ambiguous
  • The brace — one diagonal turns a wobbly frame rigid

That's why every truss, crane arm, and bridge girder you've ever seen is built from triangles.
